About Exaryn

Exaryn is a one-person research & development studio run by Chinmay Purohit in Melbourne. It started as a home for side projects — AI agents one month, derivatives pricing the next, a webcam party game somewhere in between — and grew into a place to build, learn, and share the results.

The approach is simple: build things, ship them, and be honest about how they went. Some projects are polished and live on the internet; others are early prototypes still finding their shape. Every one of them taught something, and they're all in the index.

The studio is happily AI-native — more than a billion Claude tokens this year and counting, turned into working software. The daily signal exists because keeping up with the AI world is a job in itself, so a robot compiles the best of it every morning.

THE THINGS THAT RECHARGE THE WORK.

  1. Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

    Chess you play with your whole body. Every position is a board state, every grip is a move that opens some lines and closes others, and the best people on the mat are always two exchanges ahead of you. It rewards leverage and timing over strength, punishes ego instantly, and asks you to solve problems while someone actively dismantles your solution.

    I haven't found better training for staying calm inside a hard problem — on the mat or in a codebase.

  2. AFL

    The game I grew up with and never grew out of. Footy is chaos with structure — momentum that can swing an entire match in a single minute, athletes covering more ground than any other code of football asks of them, and eighty thousand people holding their breath over one bounce of a genuinely unpredictable ball.

    Winter weekends in Melbourne are built around it, and I wouldn't have it any other way.

  3. Hiking

    The best debugger I own doesn't run on a laptop. A few hours on a trail does something screens can't: problems quietly untangle themselves somewhere around the second kilometre, and whatever felt urgent gets resized to how big it actually is. I've walked into a hike stuck and walked out with the answer more times than I can count.

  4. The gym

    The most honest feedback loop in my week. Progress in software can be ambiguous — the gym never is. The weight goes up or it doesn't, and either way it tells you the truth. An hour of lifting is moving meditation: one clear input, total focus, and a reset button for whatever the day did before it.

  5. Reading

    Someone spends a decade learning something, then two more years writing it down — and you get all of it for the price of a paperback and a few evenings. Books are the highest-leverage entertainment there is: a good one rewires how you think, and a great one does it while you're enjoying yourself too much to notice.

    Paper only. The battery never dies.

  6. A proper latte

    Small, daily, done properly. A well-made latte is a little piece of craftsmanship you get to hold — good beans, milk textured just right, made by someone who cares about the difference. Living in Melbourne sets that bar unreasonably high, and I've stopped apologising for caring about it.

    It's the warm pause that starts the thinking.
